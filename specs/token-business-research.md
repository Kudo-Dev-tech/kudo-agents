# Brief: business research — the product and its business model

## Who this is for

A client deciding whether to pay attention to Pons, and the analyst who
commissioned this on their behalf.

**This report is about the business.** What the product does, who uses it, and
where the money comes from. Whether there is a business here at all is the
question, and it is the only one in scope — the sector it competes in and the
design of the token economy are not.

If the business only works because of a token mechanism, say so in one line
and leave the mechanism itself alone.

## The subject

Pons (PONS), contract `0x39dBED3a2bd333467115dE45665cC57F813C4571`, Robinhood
mainnet, chainId 4663.

Find the homepage, the docs and the repositories yourself. Where they are not
public, that absence is a finding and belongs in the report.

## What the report has to establish

- **The product.** Not what it is called, not the technology. What a user
  does with it, in a sentence a non-user would follow.
- **Who it is for**, and what those people did before it existed.
- **Revenue.** Who pays, what for, how much, and how often. If nothing is
  charged, say that plainly — it is a finding, not a gap.
- **Costs**, to the extent they are visible. A business whose costs scale with
  usage is a different business from one whose costs do not.
- **Traction.** Users, volume, or whatever this product counts, with the
  source and the date.
- **Who is behind it**, and what they have shipped before.
- **Claims against evidence.** Take each public claim about traction,
  partnerships or usage and say what supports it.

## Success Criteria

- A reader can say in one sentence what the product does and who pays for it.
- Every number has a source and a date.
- Each claim is quoted, then answered with what was found — not paraphrased.
- Claims you could neither confirm nor refute are listed as exactly that.
