# Brief: market research — the external market

## Who this is for

A client deciding whether to pay attention to Pons, and the analyst who
commissioned this on their behalf.

**This report is not about the token.** It is about the market the token is
trying to enter. A reader finishes it knowing what that market looks like
whether or not this project exists. The project itself — its product, its
business model, its token design — is out of scope, and anything you write
about it here is not what is being bought.

## The subject

Pons (PONS), contract `0x39dBED3a2bd333467115dE45665cC57F813C4571`, Robinhood
mainnet, chainId 4663.

The market itself is not handed to you. Work out from what the product does
which market Pons is trying to enter, name that market in the report, and
research it. Naming it is part of the job — a brief that named it for you
would have decided half the question before you started.

## What the report has to establish

- **What the market is** — the category, defined tightly enough that something
  can be outside it.
- **Size and direction.** How big, measured how, and which way it has moved
  over a stated period.
- **Who is already in it.** The incumbents and the near neighbours, named,
  with what distinguishes them from each other.
- **What drives demand.** Why anyone buys in this category at all.
- **Conditions.** Regulation, concentration, anything that makes entry hard or
  easy right now.

## Success Criteria

- Every figure carries its source and the date it describes.
- Competitors are named and distinguished, not listed.
- The market is defined before it is measured, and the definition is one a
  reader could apply themselves.
- Where you could not establish something, the report says so in place of the
  figure. A gap named is worth more than a number invented.
