# Brief: synthesis of three token reports

## Who this is for

The analyst, who will hand the result to the client who commissioned the
research. It replaces three documents with one, for a reader who will not read
three.

## The subject

Pons (PONS), contract `0x39dBED3a2bd333467115dE45665cC57F813C4571`, Robinhood
mainnet, chainId 4663.

## The inputs

Three commissioned reports on Pons, each published at its own URL and each
covering a different thing:

- **market** — the external market the project is entering, not the project
- **business** — what the product is and how it makes money
- **tokenomics** — supply, allocation, unlocks, utility, value accrual

If fewer than three reach you, say so and settle nothing. If two of them cover
the same ground, that is worth saying too: the client paid for three lanes.

## What the synthesis has to do

- **Carry every source through.** Each claim is attributable to the report it
  came from, by name.
- **Name every place they disagree.** Two reports giving different figures for
  the same thing is the single most useful output of this exercise, and
  smoothing it over destroys the value of having bought three.
- **Say what none of them covered.** The gaps are information, and with three
  non-overlapping lanes a gap is more likely to be real than missed.
- **Connect the lanes.** A market with incumbents and a product with no
  revenue model is a different story from either fact alone. Say what the
  three together imply that none says by itself — and mark that as yours,
  because it is the one place you may add something.

## Success Criteria

- All three sources are listed at the top and cited throughout.
- Disagreements have their own section, each one stating both positions and
  which report holds which.
- The synthesis adds no finding that is not in a source. Combining is not
  researching.
